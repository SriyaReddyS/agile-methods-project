# Animal dataset audit

Reviewed November 20, 2026 on `feature/expand-to-50-animal-profiles`. The directory contains 50 profiles with 50 distinct scientific names. Five profiles cover subspecies. Each status applies to the named taxon.

## Taxon and profile verification

The table links directly to global IUCN assessments. Assessment dates and publication years are recorded separately because amended assessments can be published years after the underlying assessment. Profile citations cover habitat, range, threats and ecological role. The 30 expanded records now include geographic summaries in place of instructions to look up the range. NOAA Fisheries, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and New Zealand’s Department of Conservation provide additional species accounts where relevant.

The Amur leopard score is from the 2008 subspecies assessment. The public IUCN index returned no newer subspecies result. The Wildlife Conservation Society account supports its current range and ecology. The green turtle’s global assessment is Least Concern even though individual populations and legal listings differ. The monarch’s global assessment is also Least Concern. IUCN assessed its migratory subspecies as Vulnerable on September 27, 2023. ## Image attribution

Added 30 JPEG photographs and checked each image against its individual Commons file record. The descriptions, taxon categories and downloaded images were used to check subject identity. Creator and license come from each file’s metadata and license declaration. Each new image has descriptive alt text and linked attribution on its card and detail page. The repository versions were oriented, resized where needed and re-encoded for the web at a maximum of 1,200 pixels on the long edge. Public-domain government photos retain their agency credits.

The original 20 media assets were retained and decoded successfully in Chromium. Their individual Commons files were checked for credit and license. The existing vaquita illustration and historical Javan rhinoceros postcard retain their media labels.

## Pittsburgh actions

- The Amur leopard actions use online WCS support and locally organized education or fundraising. The profile explains the scope of general donations. [WCS Get Involved](https://www.wcs.org/get-involved)
- The hawksbill actions cover refusing tortoiseshell purchases, reducing litter and joining Pittsburgh-area cleanup events. [NOAA Hawksbill Turtle](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/hawksbill-turtle) · [Allegheny CleanWays land-based cleanups](https://www.alleghenycleanways.org/land-based-cleanups.html)
- The Sumatran orangutan actions cover checking products for certified palm oil, contacting retailers and supporting the Sumatran Orangutan Society remotely. [Sumatran Orangutan Society palm oil guidance](https://www.orangutans-sos.org/learn/palm-oil/)
- The monarch profile gives Pittsburgh residents a planting action using regional native milkweed and nectar plants. [USFWS monarch account](https://www.fws.gov/species/monarch-danaus-plexippus)
- The other 46 profiles link to Pittsburgh Zoo & Aquarium for general conservation education and participation. [Pittsburgh Zoo & Aquarium conservation](https://www.pittsburghzoo.org/conservation/)

## Failures found and fixes

- The three original profiles that store `protectionActions` showed no Pittsburgh section, and the validator required a newer optional field on those profiles. The renderer and validator now accept the original action shape.
- The Marine & Coastal filter matched only coral reef prose. The filters now use explicit habitat values. Near Threatened and Least Concern can also be selected.
- The directory heading described every profile as endangered, despite the range of assessed categories. The heading and introduction now cover the full directory.
- Chromium reported a 404 for its default favicon request. The pages now declare an empty favicon.
- A dependency install initially could not write to its default cache. Re-running the locked install with a workspace cache allowed the complete automated suite to run.
